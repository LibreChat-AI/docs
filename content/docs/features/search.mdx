Message search finds text across every conversation you have had, so you can get back to that one answer from three weeks ago without scrolling through the sidebar. It is backed by Meilisearch, which matches as you type and tolerates small typos.

## Searching

Type in the **Search messages** box in the sidebar, or press Ctrl+/ (⌘ / on macOS) to jump to it. Results load a moment after you stop typing. The main area lists matching messages with a count, each with the title of its conversation; click the title to open that chat. At the same time the chat list in the sidebar narrows to conversations that match, either by title or tags or because one of their messages does.

Press Escape, or delete the last character, to clear the search. That also leaves the results page and starts a new chat.

To narrow the chat list by date, endpoint, attachments, sharing, or bookmarks without typing a query, use the [chat list filters](/docs/features/navigation#filter-and-sort-the-chat-list) instead.

## What is searchable

Search covers conversation titles and tags, and the text of your own messages and the assistant's replies. You never see results from other users' conversations.

It matches the words you type, not their meaning. Searching "banana" finds messages that contain "banana", not a message about fruit that never uses the word. Meaning-based retrieval in LibreChat only applies to uploaded files, through [file search](/docs/features/rag_api).

Temporary chats are not indexed, so they never show up in results. New messages are indexed shortly after they are saved; on a large existing database the first full index can take a while after search is turned on, and older conversations appear as it catches up.

## Setup

Search is off until an admin runs Meilisearch and sets `SEARCH=true`; without it the search box does not appear. See [Meilisearch](/docs/configuration/meilisearch) for the Docker and source setups and for re-syncing the index.
