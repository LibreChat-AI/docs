This page is for everyone who talks to an agent in Slack. If the bot is already in your workspace, you can be up and running in about a minute.

## Start a conversation

<Tabs items={['In a channel', 'In a direct message']}>
<Tabs.Tab>

Invite the bot once per channel, then mention it with your question:

```text
/invite @support_assistant
@support_assistant How do I reset a customer's password?
```

Installing the app does not add it to every channel. If nothing happens when you mention it, invite it first.

</Tabs.Tab>
<Tabs.Tab>

Find the bot under **Apps** in Slack's sidebar, open it, and send a message on the **Messages** tab. No mention is needed in a direct message.

</Tabs.Tab>
</Tabs>

The bot answers in a thread under your message.

## Link your account

A successful answer means you are set up. To check tool access too, ask the agent to use a tool you are allowed to use.

<Callout type="info" title="Linking does not grant access">
Your role must allow using Agents and Remote Agents, and the agent's owner must grant you **Remote Access** (normal sharing is not enough). Linking only proves who you are. If the bot says **Your account is linked, but this channel has not authorized this request**, ask the agent's owner or your administrator for access.
</Callout>

The link prompt is valid for 10 minutes, and a link lasts 30 days by default. When it expires, the bot asks you to link again.

## Follow up in a thread

- **In a channel, mention the bot in your reply.** The bot only receives messages that mention it, so `@support_assistant and for admins?` in the thread continues the conversation, while a reply without the mention is not sent to the agent.
- **In a direct message, reply in the thread.** Each new top-level message starts a new conversation; replies in its thread continue it.
- **Everyone has their own conversation.** If a teammate asks in the same thread, the agent answers them separately, with their account. You cannot see their approvals or continue their conversation, and they cannot continue yours.
- **The agent does not read the thread.** It remembers your own earlier questions and answers in that thread, but not other people's messages. Quote anything it needs to know.
- **One request at a time.** If you ask again before the first answer arrives, your second request starts when the first one finishes.

## While the agent works

A progress message appears in the thread, starting with **⏳ Working…**, and updates as the run moves on.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Stop** | Asks LibreChat to cancel your request. The message changes to **Cancellation requested.** Work a tool already finished is not undone. |
| **Review request** | Appears when the agent needs your input. Opens a private prompt that only you can see. |

Only the person who sent a request can stop or review it. When the answer is delivered, the progress message is removed. If the run is cancelled or fails, the message stays with its final status.

### Questions and approvals

When the agent asks a question or a tool needs your approval, select **Review request**:

- **Short questions** show their choices as buttons in a private message, plus **Write an answer**.
- **Longer questions** open an **Agent needs your input** form. Choose or write an answer and select **Send answer**.
- **Tool approvals** show the tool call with **Decision for this call only**: **Approve** or **Decline**. Each decision applies to that one call. To always allow a tool, change the agent's approval settings in LibreChat.
- **Sign-ins and other forms** open LibreChat in your browser with **Review in LibreChat** (your deployment's name may appear instead of LibreChat). Nothing is approved in Slack for these.

After you answer, Slack confirms with a short private note, such as **Tool call approved.** That confirms your decision was received, not that the tool has finished. Approvals expire after 24 hours by default.

## Answers

The final answer is posted as a reply in the thread. Slack formatting is applied automatically:

- **Text** keeps headings, lists, bold and italics, links, and code blocks.
- **Tables** in the agent's answer become Slack tables.
- **Files** the agent creates are uploaded to the thread, up to 5 files of 10 MB each by default.
- **Long or code-heavy answers** are also attached as `agent-response.md` so you can download the full text.
- **Charts, cards, and carousels** appear when the agent uses the [rich message format](/docs/features/agent_channels/rich_messages).

Each answer ends with a small footer showing the cost (when available), the time taken, and a short run ID to share with your administrator if something looks wrong. When you have asked several questions in a thread, the footer also totals them.

Very long answers are shortened with the note **Reply shortened to fit this channel's delivery limit.** The full answer is still saved in LibreChat.

## Give feedback

React to an answer with 👍 or 👎 to rate it. When feedback collection is enabled on your deployment, the reaction is recorded for that answer. Removing the reaction removes the rating.

## Native agent sessions

Slack can show an agent's status (such as thinking or waiting for input) in its own interface, with Slack's built-in Stop button and **Helpful** / **Not helpful** buttons on answers. This is optional and needs both Slack and LibreChat to be set up for it:

1. In your Slack app, enable the agent features and add the bot scope that Slack's [agent sessions](https://docs.slack.dev/ai/agent-sessions/) guide requires (`assistant:write` at the time of writing), and subscribe to the `agent_session_stopped` event. Manual connections already include the event.
2. Reinstall the app in Slack (organization apps: **Reconnect** in LibreChat).
3. The operator sets `nativeSessions: true` under `endpoints.agents.channels` in `librechat.yaml`.

If the permission or event is missing, the bot falls back to the regular progress message.

The operator can also set `lifecycleReactions: true` to add reactions to your message as it is processed: 👀 and ⏳ while working, then ✅ when done or ⚠️ on failure. A reaction only shows progress; it does not prove that a tool finished.

## Manage your connection

Your links are listed in LibreChat under **Settings → Account → Your channel account links**. Each row shows the agent, your Slack user and workspace IDs, and the link status.

<ThemeImage
  light="/images/features/agent_channels/settings-account-links-light.png"
  dark="/images/features/agent_channels/settings-account-links-dark.png"
  alt="Your channel account links in Account settings, showing the Support Assistant agent with Slack user and workspace IDs"
  width={594}
/>

To disconnect, hover over or focus the row, select **Disconnect**, and confirm. The bot will ask you to link again next time. Actions the agent already completed are not undone.

| Link status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| In progress | You opened the link but have not linked it yet. |
| Awaiting confirmation | Linked in the browser; select **Confirm account** in Slack. |
| Active | Ready to use. |
| Expired | The link reached its lifetime. Mention the bot to link again. |
| Revoked | You or an administrator disconnected it. |
| Unavailable | The connection changed or was disabled. Mention the bot to link again. |

## Tips for better results

- **Send text, not attachments.** Files and images are not passed to the agent, and a message with an attachment may be ignored. Paste the relevant text instead.
- **Be specific in the first message.** The agent sees only your message, not the surrounding conversation.
- **Start a new thread for a new topic.** A fresh top-level mention gives the agent a clean context.
- **Use direct messages for personal work.** Answers in channels are visible to everyone there.
- **Share the run ID** from the footer when you report a problem.

Something not working? See [Troubleshooting](/docs/features/agent_channels/slack/troubleshooting).
